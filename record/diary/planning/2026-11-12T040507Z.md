# planning, 2026-11-12 04:05Z: the change to the rules that R17 calls for (version 3)

This PR's own entry. It changes `record/COMMANDMENTS.txt` and nothing else but this entry.

**Why.** In R17 the owner chose a record thread for the coordinator: "A new standing thread is the
coordinator's hands in the repo: it writes rulings.md, player-diary.md and the coordinator's diary in
your exact words and builds the step zips." The rules name only the coordinator for that writing and
building. A ruling that changes what the rules say binds from its entry, and the planning thread writes
the change at once (rule 1). Why I read R17 that way is in `2026-11-12T040012Z.md`, which is in the
record-only PR from branch `claude/planning-rulings`.

**What changed, place by place.** Seven lines of the file are changed and one paragraph is added.

1. The header: version 3, ratified 2026-11-12, replaces version 2 (2026-11-11). The date is the day of
   the owner's ruling in R17, by the clock the record keeps (UTC).
2. THE CAST, a new paragraph after the coordinator's. It says what the record thread is (a standing
   thread, the coordinator's hands in the repo, role name "record"), what it writes (rulings.md,
   player-diary.md and the coordinator's diary) and that it builds the zips rule 3 gives the coordinator.
   One sentence then covers every other place where the rules have the coordinator write in the repo or
   build a zip, so that those places need no change of their own.
3. THE CAST, the deputy sentence: a thread the owner speaks to takes itself as deputized "unless the
   owner says the coordinator will record it or the coordinator has a record thread (below)".
4. THE RECORD: a diary folder is written only by its thread, "a deputy's entries in the coordinator's
   diary, and a record thread's writing there, aside".
5. Rule 1, twice: a stop for everyone holds "every thread but itself, its record thread and the review
   thread", and the rule "never stops the review thread or a record thread".
6. Rule 5: the record thread "keeps no folder and no map of its own, and its own entries go in the
   coordinator's diary and say that they are its own".

**Where the new words go past the owner's card, and why.** The card is one sentence. These five points
are not in it. Each is here because leaving it unsaid would be a rule 1 stop the first time it came up.
The owner and the review thread can strike any of them.

- *It reads the repo for the coordinator and sends it each new entry word for word.* The coordinator
  cannot read the repo either (R16), and the rules have it send every entry of rulings.md and every
  finding to the threads they concern.
- *It holds no office of its own.* It decides nothing, relays nothing, wakes no one and changes nothing
  but the coordinator's files. Without this a second thread would stand in the second tier with no limit
  on what it may do.
- *No stop holds it.* It is the thread that records the ruling which ends a stop.
- *It keeps no folder and no map of its own.* The card has it write the coordinator's diary. Rule 5 would
  otherwise give it a folder as well, and the two would disagree.
- *A thread the owner speaks to is no longer deputized where there is a record thread.* It sends the
  owner's words to the coordinator and to the record thread, which records them. The card's second
  option was "Each thread you speak to", and the owner chose the first over it. The reason on the card
  was that one writer keeps the entries in order and no thread acts on a ruling it recorded itself. If
  the deputy sentence stood unchanged, two threads would record the same ruling.

**Why not another way.**
- Not a new tier or a new numbered rule: the record thread decides nothing, so it needs no rank of its
  own, and one paragraph beside the coordinator's keeps the change small.
- Not an edit at each of the dozen places that say "the coordinator writes" or "the coordinator records":
  one sentence in the cast covers them, and a later reader has one place to look.
- The three exceptions (the diary folder, the stop, the deputy) are written where their rules are, not
  only in the cast, because two rules that seem to disagree are a stop for everyone.
- Nothing else in the rules is touched. R5, R6 and R8 are readings of how the rules apply to this project
  and change no words (`2026-11-12T031846Z.md`).

**Code this change makes wrong** (rule 4 has the review thread list it): none that I can find. The
change is about who writes the record and builds the zips. Nothing outside `record/` reads or depends
on either.

**Until it merges,** R17 is what binds (rule 1), and the coordinator sends that entry to every thread
that starts work.
