when another walker may come: "A return that falls due waits for its target's rest, as any haunt does,
and is then served before the day's roll for a new walker. So a return reaches no player sooner than 5

**Why it is in the book.** The paragraph sets the measures of "Faster" side by side for the first
question of the brief of feature 8: the two seasons of the return, and the week after which a new walker
may come. A reader takes from it that the return time is the one number to shorten. As version 1.1.1
ships that is not so: however soon a return falls due, the player it goes to still has the rest between
haunts, and that rest begins when the walker dies in its haunt. Whoever answers the first question
needs that fact beside the other two. Why now, and not at the book's next change: a change to the
concept costs the review thread's read and the owner's merge each time (rule 4), and this one was not
yet reported ready.

**Checked in the code** (`Walkers/` on main is the code of version 1.1.1):

- `Walkers/Director/DirectorSystem.Tick.cs`, `EndHauntCore`: when a haunt ends, its victim rests for
  `director.minDaysBetweenHaunts`, 5 in-game days as shipped (the default is in
  `Walkers/Config/WalkersConfig.Director.cs`), and twice that when the victim died. A walker's death
  ends its haunt (`GoDown` in `DirectorSystem.cs`).
- `StartHaunts`, in the same file: returns that are due are served first, and each asks of its target
  what any haunt asks, that the target's rest is over. The code's own comment: "Like any haunt, a return
  waits for its target's rest; /walkers director return now skips it." The daily roll for a new walker
  comes third in that function, after the returns.
- The target of a return is the most-marked carrier of furs who has no walker of their own, or else
  its last victim. Point 7 of the concept already has that.
