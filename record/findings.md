## G7. Flaw: the deputy text does not cover a record thread that is not there

- Rank: flaw. Step named: step 1. Owning thread: planning.
- Found 2026-11-12 04:40Z in PR #50 at `551c4d6`, THE CAST: the coordinator's paragraph and the new
  paragraph after it.

Three things, in one place:
1. Two sentences disagree. Unchanged from version 2: "If there is no coordinator, or it is absent,
   unavailable or busy, the owner is the coordinator, and the thread the owner is speaking to is
   deputized". New: "Where there is a record thread, a thread to which the owner gives a ruling, a
   command or play feedback directly is not deputized", and it "is deputized only if the owner tells it
   to record them itself". With a record thread and a busy coordinator, the first says deputized and the
   second says not.
2. The condition is said two ways: "unless ... the coordinator has a record thread", and "Where there is
   a record thread". The new paragraph opens "Where the coordinator cannot reach the repo, it has a
   record thread", which is true by rule from the day version 3 merges, whether or not such a thread is
   running. A thread cannot see whether the record thread is at hand, any more than it can see the
   coordinator, which is the reason version 2 gives for having deputies at all.
3. There is no fallback. Version 2's deputy made sure the owner's word was recorded with the coordinator
   away. The draft switches the deputy off wherever a record thread exists, and puts that one thread in
   the path of every ruling: a stop ends only when the entry is in rulings.md and the coordinator has
   sent it (rule 1). If the record thread has ended or run out of room, nobody may record. The draft
   does not say who starts it or replaces it. The janitor has such a sentence, and a builder's
   replacement "takes over the name and the diary".

A possible correction, in substance. Say the condition once, as a fact a thread can know: the coordinator
has told the threads that its record thread has started. Give the first sentence the same exception. Say
that the coordinator starts the record thread and its replacement. Keep the deputy as the fallback: such
a thread is deputized if the owner tells it to record the words itself, or if the coordinator tells it
that the record thread is not at hand. The coordinator can see whether a thread is running. The threads
cannot.

Why a flaw and not a blocker: the draft's intent is plain, and the gap bites only when the record thread
or the coordinator is away.

## G9. Flaw: the record thread may neither stop nor decide, so a doubt of its own has nowhere to go

- Rank: flaw. Step named: step 1. Owning thread: planning.
- Found 2026-11-12 04:40Z in PR #50 at `551c4d6`, rule 1: "This rule never stops the review thread or a
  record thread."

The first edit to rule 1 is right: a stop for everyone must not hold the thread that records the ruling
which ends it. The second edit goes further. For the review thread, "never stops" comes with an outlet:
"What it finds is a finding, never a rule 1 stop" (rule 6). The record thread gets the same exemption
and no outlet, and the cast says "it decides nothing". When it meets two readings of what to write (is
this a ruling or a remark, one entry or two, which of two versions of the coordinator's words), the
draft lets it neither stop nor choose. It is also left freer than the coordinator it serves, which rule 1
exempts only from a stop for everyone.

The record thread showed the right practice on its first day
(`record/diary/record/2026-11-12T043000Z.md`): "I ask the coordinator whether it holds me, and write
nothing meanwhile but the stop's own entries the coordinator sends me." The text should say that. A
possible correction: leave the last sentence as version 2 has it, and add that no stop keeps a record
thread from recording, and that a doubt of its own over what to write goes to the coordinator, the
entry in doubt staying unwritten until the coordinator answers.
